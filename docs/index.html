Read this first

This site exists because a 14K or 18K stamp is not proof. Scams, plated rings labeled "gold", and mis-stamped alloys are routine on marketplaces. Our rule on this project is simple:

  • No product is added to the evidence table unless we can cite a source URL.
  • No price or weight is transcribed from memory.
  • The live gold spot price is embedded from Kitco, not copied as text, so we can't misquote it.
  • The calculator does the math from numbers you provide — it does not invent a spot price.

Gold ring — online buying checklist

  1. Gold purity: Confirm the karat — 10K, 14K, 18K, etc. Higher karat = more gold content.
  2. Solid gold: Listing must explicitly say solid gold, not plated, vermeil, gold-filled, gold overlay, or gold-tone.
  3. Hallmark: Look for a 10K / 14K / 18K stamp plus a maker’s / manufacturer’s mark (e.g. JA, BE, BN, or a registered trademark).
  4. Weight: Get the ring’s weight in grams. A legitimate seller publishes it. If they can’t, that is a yellow flag.
  5. Seller reputation: Research independent reviews and complaints; verify the business has real contact information (physical address, working phone, real domain).
  6. Price sanity check: Compare the listed price to (grams × karat-fraction × spot price per gram). Anything wildly below the metal’s melt value is almost certainly fake, plated, or wrong-weight.
  7. Return policy: You must be able to return the ring after you receive and inspect it. Watch for exclusions on resized or custom rings — they often void your ability to test before resizing.
  8. Payment: Pay by credit card for chargeback protection. Avoid wire transfer, crypto, gift cards, or any irreversible method.
  9. Documentation: Save the listing snapshot, the receipt, the seller’s text, and any claim about purity. Screenshot the date.
  10. After delivery, before resizing/wearing: Take the ring to an independent jeweler and ask them to verify the gold purity and weight.
  11. Best verification: Ask for XRF testing ($50–$150 typical — see volity.io) or another professional gold-purity test. Do not rely on the stamp alone.
  12. If the seller won’t let you independently verify or return it: do not buy it.

Findings from the deep search

After pulling the public product pages of the largest US online jewelers, the most important finding is this:

Most major US online jewelers do NOT publish the ring's weight in grams on their public product pages. They list karat, band width (in millimeters), ring size, and stone carat weight — but not the metal mass. This is exactly the "yellow flag" the checklist calls out. We have to ask the seller for it, or weigh the ring ourselves after delivery.

However, we found two exceptions: Ritani (US-based, NYC) publishes the metal weight in grams on each product detail page (e.g. The Delia classic solitaire at 4.04g, the Maddie octagon solitaire at 3.84g, the Andy eight-prong at 4.42g). David's House of Diamonds (US-based Shopify store) publishes "Gold Weight" in grams on every product page (e.g. oval solitaire at 2.8g). These sellers demonstrate that publishing the ring's gram weight is technically feasible — the other major sellers simply choose not to.

  • James Allen — karat ✓, SKU ✓, weight in grams ✗
  • Brilliant Earth — karat ✓, width ✓, weight in grams ✗
  • Kay Jewelers (Signet) — karat ✓, weight in grams ✗
  • Zales (Signet) — karat ✓, weight in grams ✗
  • Jared (Signet) — karat ✓, weight in grams ✗
  • Clean Origin — karat ✓, weight in grams ✗
  • Tiffany & Co. (LVMH) — karat ✓, hallmark ✓, weight in grams ✗ on category page
  • Taylor & Hart — karat ✓, weight in grams ✗
  • Ritani — karat ✓, width ✓, weight in grams ✓ (US-based, publishes metal weight on each product detail page; e.g. The Delia at 4.04g, The Maddie at 3.84g, The Andy at 4.42g)
  • David's House of Diamonds — karat ✓, width ✓, gold weight in grams ✓ (US-based Shopify seller; publishes "Gold Weight" in grams on every product page; e.g. oval solitaire at 2.8g)
  • The Karat Store (India, BIS-hallmarked) — karat ✓, hallmark ✓, gross weight + gold weight in grams ✓ (rare exception)

Practical implication: if a buyer's primary defense against fake or mis-stamped gold is the price-to-melt-value sanity check, they have to either (a) request the gram weight from the seller before purchase, or (b) weigh the ring on receipt, before any resizing.

A "14K" stamp on a Tiffany ring and a "14K" stamp on a cheap Etsy ring carry the same legal weight. The only way to tell the difference is XRF after delivery.

After it arrives — independent verification

A stamp is a claim, not a test. After delivery, before resizing or wearing, take the ring to an independent jeweler and ask for:

  • Weight verification on a calibrated scale (in grams).
  • Stamp inspection under magnification (purity + maker’s mark).
  • XRF (X-ray fluorescence) test — non-destructive, typically $50–$150 per item, takes seconds. [source: volity.io]
  • Stone inspection if applicable (GIA / IGI / AGS reports).

Fire assay is more accurate but destructive — usually only used on scrap gold, not on a ring you want to keep.

Sources: XRF cost ranges from volity.io and riselaser.net; the Reddit thread at r/Etsy shows what “seller demands XRF” disputes look like in real life.
